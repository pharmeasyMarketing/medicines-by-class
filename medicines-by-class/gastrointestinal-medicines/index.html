About Gastrointestinal Medicines

This class groups medicines used for the stomach and intestines. It includes antacids and acid-reducing medicines, anti-emetics for nausea, antispasmodics for cramps, and preparations used for altered bowel habit.

Ongoing digestive symptoms are worth investigating rather than managing indefinitely with over-the-counter relief. Persistent acidity, unexplained weight loss or blood in the stool should always be reviewed by a doctor.

Sub-classes you will find here

Within this class you will find Anti-Spasmodics, Anti-Emetic, Prebiotic and Probiotic, Laxative, Antacid, Antacid and Anti-Emetic, Anti-Diarrhoeal, Digestants, Antacid and Anti-Flatulent, Antacid and Anti-Reflux, Appetite Stimulant, Anal Fissure, Anti-Flatulent and Digestants and Ulcerative Colitis. Two medicines from different sub-classes are not alternatives to each other.

Prescription and dosage

Medicines in this class are prescription-only unless the pack states otherwise. Always order the exact strength and dosage form written on your prescription, and continue any monitoring your doctor has advised.

Safety information

  • Long-term use of acid-reducing medicines should be reviewed periodically by a doctor rather than continued indefinitely.
  • Do not self-medicate. Use medicines only as advised by a qualified doctor.
  • Check the strength and dosage form on your prescription before ordering.
  • Tell your doctor about allergies and about every other medicine or supplement you take.
  • Do not stop a prescribed medicine without medical advice, even if you feel better.
